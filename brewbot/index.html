Four hops, all real software. Wispr turns voice into text, Claude turns the text into a tool call, the connector checks who's asking, and the Pi turns the call into motors and heat.

Dose and grind
The chosen hopper's auger turns until the 1 kg load cell reads the exact grams. The beans drop into the grinder, and Shelly plug 1 switches it on for the grind.

    
Heat
The Stagg kettle holds the temperature. The Pi reads its own probe in the water and won't pour if it's more than 1 °C off. Shelly plug 2 is the kettle's hard kill switch.

    
Pour
The cradle tilts the kettle while the dripper turns and the rail moves the spout in and out, which makes the spiral. The 5 kg cell under the cup sets the flow, the bloom, the pulses and the stop.

  


  
Two ways to build it

  
Neither uses a robot arm. A $3,000 desktop arm can carry only 600 g, and a full kettle weighs 1.5 kg. Tilting or pumping is cheaper, can't drop anything, and takes about two weekends.

  

    

      Recommended
      
A · Tilt a temperature-controlled kettle

      
$991

      
The kettle sits in a cradle hinged near its base, and a 100:1 geared stepper leans it forward to pour. The hinge takes the weight, so the motor only needs about 9 kg·cm. The gearbox isn't self-locking, so if power drops a spring pulls the kettle back upright.

      
A real gooseneck gives a smooth stream at exactly the right temperature, and it looks great on camera.

    

    

      Cheapest to hack
      
B · Regular kettle, mixed down to temperature

      
$952

      
Any kettle boils the water, and a servo finger presses its switch to reboil. Two peristaltic pumps blend boiling and room-temperature water until a probe in the mixing cup reads the target: hot × (100 − T) = cold × (T − 25).

      
It works with any kettle and never moves anything hot. It's reliable from 85 to 94 °C. Reaching 97 °C takes a warm-up flush, because the tubing loses 3–6 °C.

    

  


  
Wiring

  
One Raspberry Pi 5 runs everything. Motors run on a separate 12 V brick through the emergency-stop loop, and anything on mains goes through certified Shelly plugs, so the build never touches mains wiring. All grounds are common.

Build your own in two weekends

  
Anyone in the office can build one. It needs no mains wiring and no special tools beyond a 3D printer, or a print service.

  
    
    
  1. Saturday 1: frame and scales. Bolt up the extrusion frame. Mount the 5 kg load cell under a plate for the cup and the 1 kg cell under the grinder chute, then calibrate both with a known weight.
    2. 
    
  2. Sunday 1: hoppers and grinder. Print three hoppers with augers and wire the gear motors to the Pi. Put the grinder on a Shelly plug. Test: "dose 18 g" should land within 0.2 g.
    3. 
    
  3. Saturday 2: the pour. Build the hinged cradle with its return spring, add the tilt stepper, the turntable and the rail. Tune the tilt against the cup scale until a pulse pours a steady 4–6 g per second.
    4. 
    
  4. Sunday 2: connector and safety. Run the MCP server on the Pi, add the cup sensor and the emergency stop, and test that no cup means no pour. Then tell your Claude about it.
    5. 
  


  
The connector

  
A small Python MCP server that runs on the Pi. It defaults to simulator mode, so you can plug it into Claude today with no hardware. It's tested: brew, status, menu and cancel all answer, and calls without the token get a 401.

  
pip install "mcp[cli]<2" uvicorn
BREWBOT_TOKEN=pick-a-secret python server.py     # simulator
BREWBOT_HW=1 BREWBOT_TOKEN=... python server.py  # on the Pi

brew(coffee, grams, temp_c, out_ml?, ratio?, bloom_s?, style?)  → brew id
status()  → stage, grams, °C, grams in the cup
menu()    → the three coffees and their default recipes
cancel()  → motors off, kettle tips upright, plugs off

  
Download server.py

  
Tell your Claude: in Claude, open Settings → Connectors → Add custom connector. Paste the rig's URL (for example https://brewbot.your-office.dev/mcp) and the token you were given. Then just say, "make me a Riverdale, 18 grams, 92 degrees." Each person gets one brew every 10 minutes, one brew runs at a time, and nothing pours without a cup on the scale. Live status shows at the same URL without /mcp.


  
What's in the hoppers

  

    
Riverdale
Riverdale Estate N72, a light roast from the Shevaroy Hills in Tamil Nadu (Blue Tokai, ₹760 for 250 g). Default: 18 g, 92–94 °C, 1:15, 45 s bloom.

    
Attikan
A medium roast for everyday cups. Default: 18 g, 92 °C, 1:16, 30 s bloom.

    
Dark
A darker roast for a heavier cup. Default: 20 g, 88 °C, 1:15, 30 s bloom.
