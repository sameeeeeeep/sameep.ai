How it works

A plain gooseneck kettle sits in a hinged cradle, and the Pi is its thermostat: a probe in the water and a smart plug on its base. A geared motor tips it to pour while the dripper turns and a rail moves the spout in and out, so the water spirals. Three hoppers dose beans by weight into a grinder. A scale under the cup runs the whole pour, and a small connector lets any Claude call brew(recipe="A1").

The full blueprint is below: drawings, wiring, the control loops, safety, the software, the build steps and every part.

Blueprint — front view (600 × 450 × 300 mm)
  1. Bean hoppers ×3, printed PETG, 250 g each, one coffee per hopper.
  2. Augers ×3, each turned by an N20 gear motor, push beans into one chute.
  3. Dose cup on the 1 kg load cell. When it reads the target, an SG90 servo tips it into the grinder.
  4. Baratza Encore ESP, on Shelly plug 1. It grinds until its power draw drops to idle, which means the beans are gone.
  5. MGN12 rail, 300 mm, belt-driven by a NEMA 17. It moves the kettle in and out to draw the spiral.
  6. Carriage and cradle hanging from the rail.
  7. Gooseneck kettle with its base, both fixed in the cradle so they tilt together. A probe goes in through the lid.
  8. Hinge and tilt motor (see the side view).
  9. V60 or Origami dripper on a fixed ring above the server.
  10. Turntable: a 6-inch lazy-susan bearing, belt-driven, spinning the server and dripper at 30–40 rpm.
  11. 5 kg load cell under the turntable. It's the scale for the whole pour.
  12. Electronics box: Pi 5, 3× TMC2209, 2× DRV8833, HX711s, 12 V input.
  13. Emergency stop, normally closed, cutting 12 V to every motor.
Side view — the tilt cradle

The kettle and its power base are clamped in an aluminium L-cradle, so they tilt together and the base contact never breaks. The hinge sits under the spout side, in front of the kettle's centre of mass. That means gravity always pulls the cradle back upright: the motor has to hold every degree of tilt, and if power drops the kettle falls back to 0°. It can't spill toward the cup.

Torque: a full 0.9 L kettle with its base weighs about 1.6 kg, and its centre of mass sits at most about 6 cm from the pivot. That needs about 9.5 kg·cm. A 17HS19 motor through a 100:1 planetary gearbox gives over 30 kg·cm, so there's about 3× margin. A printed hard stop at 40° stops it ever tipping past a full pour.

Temperature — how the Pi sets “93 °C”

The honest problem: no smart kettle you can buy today lets another device set its temperature. Fellow's kettles only listen to Fellow's app. The older Stagg EKG+ was reverse-engineered over Bluetooth (the fellow-stagg-ble library can set its target temperature), but it's discontinued, and the current EKG Pro is only partly cracked.

So the Pi becomes the kettle's thermostat. Use a plain electric gooseneck with a mechanical switch and a boil cut-off. Leave its switch on, and put its base on Shelly plug 2. A food-safe DS18B20 probe sits in a stainless sleeve through the lid, 2 cm above the bottom. The Pi reads it every 0.75 s and switches the plug:

target = 93                                   # from brew(recipe="A1")
loop every 0.75 s:
    t = probe.read()
    if t < target - 3:      kettle(on)          # full power
    elif t < target - 0.5:  pulse(2 s on, 6 s off)  # creep up, no overshoot
    else:                   kettle(off)         # hold: it loses ~1 °C a minute
    if t > 101 or rising > 3 °C/s:  kettle(off); abort("boil-dry")

kettle(on) = GET http://shelly-kettle.local/rpc/Switch.Set?id=0&on=true&toggle_after=10

Two details make this safe and accurate:

  • toggle_after=10 tells the Shelly to switch itself off after 10 seconds unless the Pi renews the command. If the Pi crashes, the kettle turns off on its own.
  • Because the Pi stops below boiling, the kettle's boil cut-off never trips, so the next "on" works. The cut-off stays in place as a second safety. The probe holds within ±1 °C, and the pour won't start unless the reading is in range.
Pour — a closed loop on the scale

The 5 kg cell under the turntable is the only thing the pour trusts. It reads 80 times a second, and a median filter turns that into grams and grams per second.

tare()                                  # cup + dripper + grounds = 0 g
bloom = 2.5 × dose                      # A1: 45 g
pour_to(bloom);  wait(bloom_s)          # A1: 45 s
for target in thirds(out_ml - bloom):   # 3 pulses
    pour_to(target);  wait(10 s)
wait until rate < 0.1 g/s               # draw-down finished → "ready"

pour_to(g):
    turntable(35 rpm); tilt to 20°, then creep until flow > 0.5 g/s
    while weight < g:
        tilt += k × (5 g/s − flow)          # hold 5 g/s whatever the kettle's level
        rail = r_max × |sin(π·t/T)|          # centre → edge → centre: the spiral
        if flow ≈ 0 for 2 s: abort("stream missing the cup")
    tilt to 0°                              # gravity helps

Why tilt is controlled by flow and not by angle: as the kettle empties, the same angle pours less and less. Closing the loop on grams per second keeps every pulse even, from a full kettle to the last 200 ml.

Dose and grind
  • Dose: the chosen auger runs fast until the 1 kg cell reads 90% of the target, then pulses in 150 ms bursts until it's within ±0.2 g. The SG90 servo then tips the cup into the grinder and returns it, and the cell re-tares.
  • Grind: the Encore ESP's dial is set once for pour-over, around 14–16. Shelly plug 1 turns it on. The plug also measures watts, so when the draw falls to idle the burrs are empty and the Pi switches it off. There's no fixed timer to guess.
  • Into the dripper: the grounds bin is swapped for a printed funnel that drops straight into the filter.
Wiring

One Raspberry Pi 5 runs everything; the probe in the kettle is on GPIO4. Motors get 12 V through the emergency-stop loop. Mains only goes through the two Shelly plugs. Common ground.

Safety — what stops a pour
  • No cup, no pour. The cup cell has to read inside the known tare window and the time-of-flight sensor has to see the server. Either one failing cancels the brew.
  • Overflow: every brew stops at target + 5%, there's an absolute ceiling of 450 g, and a stream that misses the cup stops within 2 s.
  • Boil-dry: the Pi cuts power above 101 °C or when the temperature rises faster than 3 °C/s (an empty kettle heats far faster). The kettle's own cut-off is the backstop.
  • The Pi dies: both plugs switch themselves off within 10 s (toggle_after), the motor drivers disable, and gravity tips the kettle upright.
  • Hands: a mushroom emergency stop cuts 12 V to every motor in hardware, and the Pi sees it on GPIO18. Mains only goes through the two certified plugs, behind an RCCB socket.
  • Strangers: the connector needs a bearer token, allows one brew per person every 10 minutes and one brew at a time, and cancel() always works.

Build it in two weekends
  1. Saturday 1, frame and scales. Cut and bolt the 2020 frame: 600 × 450 × 300 mm. Mount the 5 kg cell under the turntable plate and the 1 kg cell under the dose cup. Calibrate both with a 500 g weight and check the drift over 10 minutes is under 0.3 g.
  2. Sunday 1, beans. Print the hoppers, augers and dose cup. Wire the N20 motors to the DRV8833s and the servo to GPIO10. Put the grinder on Shelly 1 and set its dial to 15. Test 20 doses of 18 g: each should land within ±0.2 g.
  3. Saturday 2, water. Build the cradle and hinge, and fit the hard stop at 40°. Put the kettle base on Shelly 2 and the probe through the lid. Tune the heat loop: from 25 °C to 93 °C with under 1 °C of overshoot. Then fit the rail and turntable.
  4. Sunday 2, pour and safety. Tune the flow loop until pulses hold 5 g/s ±0.5. Wire the emergency stop and the cup sensor, and test each failure on purpose: pull the cup mid-pour, kill the Pi, run the kettle empty. Then run server.py with BREWBOT_HW=1 and add it to Claude.

The connector

A small Python MCP server for the Pi. It starts in simulator mode, so it works with no hardware. Calls without the token get a 401.

pip install "mcp[cli]<2" uvicorn
BREWBOT_TOKEN=pick-a-secret python server.py     # simulator
BREWBOT_HW=1 BREWBOT_TOKEN=... python server.py  # on the Pi

brew(recipe="A1")  or  brew(coffee, grams, temp_c, out_ml?, bloom_s?)
status()  → stage, grams, °C, grams in the cup
menu()    → the three coffees and their default recipes
cancel()  → motors off, kettle tips upright, plugs off

Download server.py

Tell your Claude: in Claude, open Settings → Connectors → Add custom connector. Paste the rig's URL (for example https://brewbot.your-office.dev/mcp) and the token you were given. Then just say, "make me a Riverdale, 18 grams, 92 degrees." Each person gets one brew every 10 minutes, one brew runs at a time, and nothing pours without a cup on the scale. Live status shows at the same URL without /mcp.
